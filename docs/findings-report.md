## Weaknesses (ranked by severity)

### 1. [HIGH] Search does not fail fast while things-search is unavailable [run: run-370897363c60] [run: run-a09cb1bcbb7c] [run: run-c8b30b07c9f3]

**Measured**
- pause_container on things-search: 8 of 74 searches failed, every one a client `ReadTimeout` after 3 s; search was out for 27.7 s [run: run-370897363c60]
- restart_container on things-search: 5 of 53 searches failed, every one a client `ReadTimeout` after 3 s; search was out for 17.1 s [run: run-a09cb1bcbb7c]
- restart_container on things-search: 2 of 14 searches failed, every one a client `ReadTimeout` after 10 s; search was out for 20.6 s [run: run-c8b30b07c9f3]

**Why (hypothesis):** The gateway holds a search request until the client gives up instead of answering with an error, so every caller waits its full timeout, and a caller without a timeout hangs. [run: run-370897363c60] [run: run-a09cb1bcbb7c] [run: run-c8b30b07c9f3]

**Recommended fix:** Bound search at the gateway: a request timeout well below the clients', or a circuit breaker on the things-search route that answers 503 at once while things-search is unavailable. [run: run-370897363c60] [run: run-a09cb1bcbb7c] [run: run-c8b30b07c9f3]

**Validation test:** Re-run the things-search restart with a fresh baseline; pass when failed searches return an error status (not a client timeout) in under one second. [run: run-370897363c60] [run: run-a09cb1bcbb7c] [run: run-c8b30b07c9f3]

### 2. [MEDIUM] Recovery after a things-search fault is slow and uneven [run: run-370897363c60] [run: run-a09cb1bcbb7c] [run: run-c8b30b07c9f3]

**Measured**
- pause_container on things-search: recovery verified 23.641 s after rollback, against 4.188 s for the fastest things-search run [run: run-370897363c60] [run: run-c8b30b07c9f3]
- restart_container on things-search: recovery verified 22.359 s after rollback, against 4.188 s for the fastest things-search run [run: run-a09cb1bcbb7c] [run: run-c8b30b07c9f3]

**Why (hypothesis):** Not measured: things-search may accept traffic before it can serve it again (readiness, cluster rejoin or index warm-up), and the recovery check waits until every SLI since rollback is back within its threshold. Two runs on different days also differ in load and timing. [run: run-370897363c60] [run: run-a09cb1bcbb7c] [run: run-c8b30b07c9f3]

**Recommended fix:** Gate traffic to things-search on a readiness check that runs a real query, and record when it reports ready, so recovery can be split into restart time and warm-up time. [run: run-370897363c60] [run: run-a09cb1bcbb7c] [run: run-c8b30b07c9f3]

**Validation test:** Re-run the pause and restart on things-search; pass when recovery is within twice the fastest run's recovery. [run: run-370897363c60] [run: run-a09cb1bcbb7c] [run: run-c8b30b07c9f3]

### 3. [LOW] The planner over-predicts impact [run: run-4083aa7e13a1]

**Measured**
- traffic_burst on nginx: freshness predicted degrade, observed hold; search predicted degrade, observed hold; write predicted degrade, observed hold [run: run-4083aa7e13a1]

**Why (hypothesis):** Every measured SLI of the run landed on the same side of its prediction. [run: run-4083aa7e13a1]

**Recommended fix:** Update the planner's effect model for this action; for a traffic burst, raise the rate within the policy's bounds until an SLI degrades, to find the real limit. [run: run-4083aa7e13a1]

**Validation test:** Re-plan and re-run with a fresh baseline; pass when the prediction matches on every measured SLI. [run: run-4083aa7e13a1]

### Fixed during testing: control-plane deadline

Faults planned at exactly the policy's maximum duration always aborted on the kill switch's deadline check, because its last poll lands just after the planned end. The action defaults now leave headroom under the cap, and no limit was loosened; the burst run below used the corrected default. Details in `docs/m5-results.md`. [run: run-4083aa7e13a1]
